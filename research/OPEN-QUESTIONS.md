## MQ-001 — Tempo entrainment: what is the effective BPM range?

**Hypothesis:** Heart rate and neural rhythms entrain to steady-beat music within a specific BPM range centered near resting heart rate.

**Current evidence:** Strong for heart rate coupling (multiple RCTs). Less clear for neural entrainment at music-tempo timescales (most EEG entrainment studies use binaural beats, not music tempo).

**Open sub-questions:**
- Is there a meaningful lower/upper bound beyond which entrainment stops?
- Does BPM need to match resting HR exactly, or is a ±10% window sufficient?
- How long does entrainment take to establish?

**Status:** open  
**Priority:** high (directly impacts vibe-state BPM recommendations)

## MQ-002 — Mode/valence: how large is the cultural and individual variance?

**Hypothesis:** Major mode correlates with positive valence; minor with negative — but this is a tendency, not a law.

**Current evidence:** Preliminary. Studies show the tendency holds in Western listeners but is substantially reduced or absent in non-Western listeners (notably Krumhansl et al., cross-cultural studies). Individual differences are large.

**Open sub-questions:**
- Which modes (Dorian, Mixolydian, Phrygian) have distinct valence profiles or are they indistinguishable from major/minor?
- Does genre context modulate the mode/valence relationship (e.g., upbeat minor dance music)?
- Is the valence effect primarily from mode or from associated melodic/harmonic conventions?

**Status:** open  
**Priority:** medium

## MQ-003 — ISO principle: does it work outside clinical music therapy?

**Hypothesis:** The ISO principle (music matching current emotional state → gradual guidance toward target state) is effective for non-clinical state-change applications.

**Current evidence:** Preliminary in clinical MT; minimal RCT data outside clinical contexts. The principle is widely used in practice but sparsely tested in consumer/AI music contexts.

**Open sub-questions:**
- What transition duration is sufficient? (30 min sessions are typical in MT; is 5 min effective?)
- Does the listener need to be aware of the principle for it to work?
- Is AI-generated music as effective as performed music for ISO applications?

**Status:** open  
**Priority:** high (foundational to vibe-os transition tool)

## MQ-004 — Binaural beats: what is the realistic effect size?

**Hypothesis:** Binaural beats in the delta (1–4 Hz) and theta (4–8 Hz) range produce measurable EEG frequency-following responses and corresponding behavioral changes.

**Current evidence:** Preliminary. EEG frequency-following is consistently reported but effect sizes vary substantially across studies. Meta-analyses show inconsistent results for anxiety and mood outcomes.

**Open sub-questions:**
- What headphone type and volume are required for a reliable effect?
- How long must exposure last before a measurable EEG shift is detectable?
- Does the carrier frequency (e.g., 200 Hz vs 400 Hz) affect outcomes?
- Are self-reported outcomes reliable here, or do they reflect expectation effects?

**Status:** open  
**Priority:** medium

## MQ-005 — Lyric efficacy: do affirmation-style lyrics measurably shift self-referential processing?

**Hypothesis:** First-person, present-tense, achievable-claim lyrics activate medial prefrontal cortex (self-referential processing network) and shift self-perception.

**Current evidence:** Preliminary. Neuroimaging shows mPFC activation for self-referential content; few RCTs on lyric-specific interventions. The closest evidence base is from affirmation psychology (Cohen & Sherman, 2014 — Strong for broad self-affirmation; Preliminary for music-delivered affirmation specifically).

**Open sub-questions:**
- Does the music delivery matter, or is the text alone sufficient?
- What repetition frequency is needed for durable effect?
- Which specific lyric structures (POV, tense, subject matter) maximize mPFC engagement?

**Status:** open  
**Priority:** low (relevant to lyric-writer agent; does not change agent behavior until answered)

## MQ-006 — Orchestration density and cognitive load

**Hypothesis:** Dense orchestral textures increase cognitive load and arousal; sparse textures decrease them — independently of tempo and mode.

**Current evidence:** Anecdotal in the orchestration literature; little controlled empirical work separating density from other musical parameters.

**Open sub-questions:**
- Can density be operationalized well enough for controlled studies (e.g., number of simultaneous voices, spectral density)?
- Does the effect hold across listener expertise levels?

**Status:** open  
**Priority:** low

## MQ-007 — AI-generated music vs performed music: equivalent state-change effect?

**Hypothesis:** AI-generated music (Suno, Udio) is as effective as studio-produced or performed music for state-change applications.

**Current evidence:** No published studies as of 2026. This is the largest empirical gap in the vibe-os framework.

**Open sub-questions:**
- Does listener knowledge that music is AI-generated change its effectiveness (expectation effect)?
- Are there specific quality artifacts in AI music (timbral flatness, structural repetition) that reduce entrainment efficacy?

**Status:** open  
**Priority:** high (directly relevant to all AI-music tools in this ecosystem)
